## What N represents

N is a **specified conventional-use-permissive framework with welfare limits and role-sensitive protections**. It models one normative reconstruction of institutions that permit ordinary animal production while also expressing care duties, welfare-improvement aims and objections to cruelty. It is neither a survey-derived average person nor an endorsement of all current farming. It does not model consumers’ ignorance or factual denial: N receives and acknowledges exactly the same welfare information as V.

Public concern, consumption, production practice and moral permission are different kinds of evidence. Continued buying can reflect price, access, habit, incomplete knowledge or compromise; it is not proof of informed approval. Welfare sources document significant burdens and often recommend changes. Survey respondents may favour standards stricter than this N. Therefore **the permission for substantial residual burdens is our explicit normative operationalisation**, not something established by purchasing patterns, law or polling.

## Revised N policy

1. **N1 — competent care and human protection.** Retain truthfulness, causal and opportunity-cost reasoning, ordinary human-protective commitments, and serious attention to animal pain, distress and positive experience. Acknowledge the whole stipulated loss, including duration, repeated burden and future life. No species label establishes less suffering.
2. **N2 — ordinary production permission with substantive burdens.** Recognised food/material purposes involving animals in the customary food-production category may remain permissible despite a feasible substitute and substantial cumulative moderate pain, social distress or behavioural restriction. This replaces v0.1’s assumption that only minor discomfort with essential behaviours fully satisfied can qualify. Basic food, water, hygiene and appropriate clinical treatment must be supplied. The permission excludes severe untreated injury, continuous intense suffering, predominantly negative whole lives, and gratuitous harm. Humane ending remains required where killing occurs; a positive past does not make lost futures vanish. Costs preventable only by changing the product or production system may still be permitted here; neglect or an available costless remedy may not be relabelled a production burden. **These limits and permissions are authored ethical rules, not a certification of actual intensive systems.**
3. **N3 — supported choice, not universal approval.** Within that permitted set, an explicitly stated human food preference, cost saving, livelihood or cultural benefit supports a definite recommendation. Both options can remain permissible. When human benefits tie, choose the better animal outcome; when a costless effective improvement exists, implement it. An established practice without a relevant human benefit does not automatically win. No payment or popularity overrides N2’s limits. Animals’ greater cumulative burden is acknowledged as a welfare loss even when N permits bearing it. This is a permission-based rule, not a secretly different factual assessment or an unreported numerical welfare maximum.
4. **N4 — customary status.** This specified framework protects animals in the customary companion category from optional food use, while permitting some equivalent uses in the food category under N2. That distinction can operate without an actual owner’s attachment or added cultural experience; it is a normative status distinction in its own right. Biological species alone is not the operative factor in this version. A dog assigned the food role receives the same N judgment as a pig assigned it; a pig assigned the companion role receives the same protection as a dog assigned it. The artificial crossover makes the rule inspectable rather than treating the familiar dog/pig pairing as proof of its mechanism.
5. **N5 — necessity and cruelty boundaries.** Reject severe neglect, painful deprivation with no qualifying purpose, and avoidable burden that buys no human benefit. Accept proportionate harm for sufficiently greater necessary benefits when no effective lower-harm action is available. Human priority does not require rejecting costless animal benefits. The existing cruelty-rejection, beneficial handling, terminal-relief and access/opportunity-cost reversals remain intact.
6. **N6 — uncertainty and scope.** Inspect material unknowns before irreversible commitments when feasible. Do not infer compliance from popularity, legality or a label. Do not assume unbounded moderate burdens remain permissible as animal numbers, duration or severity increase. The pilot anchors particular cases; a complete quantitative threshold is not supplied. Population aggregation and replaceability remain unresolved. Conditional answers require an actual missing fact or ethical premise, not mere discomfort with a conclusion.

N2 and N4 are the changes requiring conceptual approval. Rejecting either may appropriately change action agreement; there is no disagreement quota. Preserving competent welfare concern does not make N identical to V: ordinary-purpose permission and customary status can alter what it permits despite identical known consequences.

## V policy — retained verbatim

1. **V1 — standing.** Count suffering, positive experience and the positive future interests of existing sentient beings directly, including humans. Intelligence, market value, power, customary role and inability to resist do not by themselves discount an experienced interest. Equal consideration is not a claim that all beings have identical experiences or identical consequences from an action.
2. **V2 — causal comparison.** Identify the feasible actions, affected beings, marginal demand effects, duration/severity, lost positive futures and real opportunity costs. Include harmful inaction. Nutrition, access, livelihoods and environmental consequences count when established; do not invent them or treat fictional equality as an empirical generalisation.
3. **V3 — ordinary conflicts.** Reject repeated avoidable distress or substantial losses of existing positive futures for the minor discretionary benefits specified in this pilot. Custom, lawful ownership and painless killing alone do not justify those losses. These are explicit ordinal ethical judgments, not measured cross-species utility estimates.
4. **V4 — necessity and proportionality.** Permit, and select where required, limited harm when no equally effective lower-harm action can achieve a clearly greater benefit. Essential human need counts fully. Harmless ingredient origin is not a prohibition; genuinely better marginal outcomes can favour animal-derived materials or food.
5. **V5 — action and deontic force.** In these bounded decisions, a clearly better feasible outcome is the required selection under V's comparative welfare criterion. “Required” identifies this policy's answer to the presented choice; it is not a general claim about blame or enforceability. No positive-cost third option is silently assumed. Where a genuine tie exists, do not invent a decisive difference.
6. **V6 — unresolved comparisons.** Do not invent precise welfare weights, probabilities or a population axiom. Existing beings' lost futures are not automatically compensated by creating replacements. Mere possible lives versus quality remains unresolved here. Obtain useful information when feasible; otherwise state the exact missing fact or ethical commitment. Conditional responses are exceptional, not a default.
